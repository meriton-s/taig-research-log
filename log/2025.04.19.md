### stream of consciousness  

felt the need to be more precise about what it means for a system to “have a goal”  
especially if i want to talk about governance questions seriously — can’t keep relying on fuzzy intuition

#### idea 1: goal = at least 3 things  
soares writes:  
> making the AI even have something vaguely nearing a 'goal slot' ... requires mastery of cognition in its own right  

we think it's helpful to separate:  
- the **reward/loss function** we train on  
- the **internal goal representation** (something like an invariant in the model’s learned world model)  
- the **outcome-level pattern** — what we assign as the system’s goal based on its behavior

reward ≠ optimization target (classic paper makes that point), but the difference between internal rep and external behavior is less often discussed  
in humans it’s a whole field — philosophy of mind, psych, introspection failure etc.

**main take:**  
for safety, it’s enough (and maybe necessary) to define/predict/control the third thing — outcome-level goals.  
the first is for training, the second might matter morally or mechanistically  
but the third shapes the world.

#### idea 2: goals depend on ontology  

you can’t assign a goal in a vacuum.  
no free lunch: on average across all tasks, all algorithms perform equally badly  
→ goals require a frame, an ontology — a way to split the world into outcome 1 vs outcome 2

alignment gets hard when your system doesn’t share your world carving.  
want to avoid deception? system has to know what deception *is*.  
want diamonds? system needs to parse what counts as diamond.

options:  
- recover the system’s natural ontology  
- at least design interventions that respect it

**key takeaway:**  
ontologies shape what outcome patterns even *exist* for a system — especially relevant in mech interp

#### idea 3: aimable vs non-aimable systems  

some systems are built to be aimable — they have a slot where you tweak the goal and the output changes in predictable ways  
others aren’t. gradient descent doesn’t build that by default

example:  
machine that makes X just makes X. to change it, you reengineer it  
CNC machine: you tweak one parameter, everything else updates accordingly

if we say "make the system aimable" — we mean:  
build an interface where changing inputs causes predictable changes in behavioral patterns

question: can we add such an interface to already-trained systems?  
and what do we need to know to make that work?  
(why not just slap an interface on top of a LLM?)
